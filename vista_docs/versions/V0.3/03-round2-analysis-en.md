## 1. Four supply routes; one curated launch catalog

The fourth mechanism matters because it provides access to **existing businesses through existing relationships**, rather than waiting for strangers or training new founders.

| Supply route | Role now |
|---|---|
| Owned/group services: Konkooria, Irancell | Controlled access to product teams and prospective users |
| Friends’ apps: Eitala, Moneyro, Amoozim, Zambil | Warm-start partner recruitment; potentially the strongest route to differentiated local services |

| «داشت» | Longer-term supplier creation; not a dependency for the first release |

**Yes, I agree with dropping open-ended MCP discovery from launch.** Round 1 gave the ecosystem end-state too much weight relative to the quick-start objective. Keep one operator-maintained catalog: the assistant searches connected services first, then other approved entries. It can suggest connecting an approved app; it cannot search arbitrary registries, admit new providers, or install packages. Requests for unavailable services become product feedback.

Keep standard MCP underneath. Using a standard does not oblige us to build an open marketplace. Registry federation, arbitrary user-added servers, and automated publication can wait.

One qualification: **international does not necessarily mean harder; financial/local does not necessarily mean easier.** A public information service can be simple; a gold or remittance partner still needs a usable integration and operating agreement. Warm relationships reduce negotiation cost, not authorization or settlement requirements. Ask each friend for an integration owner, supported workflow, test access, and access to willing pilot users—not just agreement in principle.

## 2. Finance is a better candidate—but narrow it to a job

### Correcting the education argument

I mischaracterized Konkooria as a course/enrollment service. Its current product documentation confirms a **continuing mentor–student relationship**, with study tracking and mentor tools; consultation payments are outside the platform and it takes no percentage of them. That makes my course-purchase example inappropriate. [L1]

Its possible advantage over a general chatbot is the actual mentoring relationship and recorded progress, not “AI teaches you things.” Amoozim could add learning content, but your description does not establish a common recurring workflow across Amoozim, Konkooria, and Google Calendar. I withdraw that bundle as the recommended starting point. Any future integration must preserve Konkooria’s existing privacy and business rules rather than assume a new payment model.

Also, **density does not require covering every need in education or finance**. It requires a particular group to complete a valuable recurring job. That is a much smaller—and more testable—target.

### My answer: yes to a financial starting point; not the whole wealth-management category

Eitala gives us a more concrete hypothesis than generic education: people already check gold prices, review holdings, calculate what an amount buys, and place orders. Vista can shorten the path from those questions to a correctly authorized action.

I checked Eitala’s local API description and small relevant models. They describe current prices, purchase/sale operations, timestamped price records, and investment cases with gold-denominated shares. This supports feasibility investigation, **not a claim that all those capabilities are production-ready APIs for Vista users**. The documented balance endpoint belongs to a bot-linked account; per-user authorization still needs an explicit design. [L2]

**First candidate cohort:** existing Eitala customers who already use gold for periodic saving. Recruit them with the partner and test this complete loop:

> Understand my holdings and today’s quote → see exactly what my chosen amount buys after charges → review the terms → authorize the order → see its receipt and status.

The differentiation is **account context + provider-backed terms + execution + follow-up**, not a claim that our model knows finance better than ChatGPT. Even that advantage must be tested against both the existing Eitala experience and assistants with connectors.

Keep Moneyro as a second candidate for remittance/international-purchase workflows. Do not assume its users overlap with gold savers merely because both products are financial. Add crowdfunding and manufacturing-investment offers only after understanding their distinct suitability, liquidity, custody, disclosure, and dispute requirements. An illiquid project investment is not another gold-buy button.

### “AI says buy or sell” should not be the initial promise

I would start with **decision support**, not confident trading signals: sourced prices and news, timestamps, fees/spreads, scenario comparisons, and explicit uncertainty. News about war or negotiations does not reliably imply a particular next price; explanations must distinguish facts from hypotheses.

Personalized buy/sell recommendations need a separate decision about validation, suitability, conflicts of interest, and the responsible licensed/advisory arrangement. A signed order proves authorization; it does not establish that advice was suitable. Eitala’s or Vista’s revenue must not depend on making the assistant encourage more trading.

**Bank integration need not block the pilot if a partner can lawfully execute and settle through its existing rails.** That does not remove the need for a payment/settlement provider. Keep custody, KYC, execution responsibility, refunds, and support explicit; every financially effective Vista-initiated action still goes through deterministic authorization, even when the funds are held outside Vista. If the partners require an unfinished bank connection, finance loses its speed advantage.

Before choosing it conclusively, verify three things: **a willing operating partner, a complete authorized transaction path, and users who demonstrably prefer the shorter workflow**. Measure task success, time/effort saved, repeat use, and support/dispute burden—not trading volume alone. Irancell’s existing service workflows remain a fallback if the financial path cannot pass those gates.

## 3. AP2 is the foundation, not just something to investigate

Your instruction changes the default: **adopt AP2 where it applies and extend only the gaps**, instead of starting from a proprietary Vista contract protocol.

| Concern | Proposed treatment |
|---|---|
| Purchase/payment authorization | AP2 checkout and payment mandates, bound to the actual merchant terms, with receipts |
| Consent presentation | Vista’s deterministic contract UI as the trusted surface |
| Initial trust arrangement | AP2’s **Trusted Agent Provider** model is a good candidate for curated partners that explicitly trust Vista |
| Nonfinancial permissions, local assurance rules, domain-specific terms | Reuse the general authorization framework where it fits; define separate, versioned extensions where necessary |
| Future unattended action | AP2-compatible constrained mandates, with Vista’s limits; not enabled merely because AP2 supports them |

AP2’s Trusted Agent Provider model allows a securely held platform key to attest consent obtained through a trusted surface; the model must not access that key or invoke signing outside that surface. This is relevant to starting with authenticated sessions/OTP without pretending they are personal cryptographic signatures. The detailed assurance mapping remains a specification task. [S1]

**Dropping “the app always signs first” is fine as a universal Vista axiom.** Replace it with: *the required parties and proof depend on the contract type; execution requires the necessary authorization over the exact terms*. But AP2’s payment flow still requires a merchant-signed checkout to which authorization is bound. Relaxing our general slogan cannot remove a requirement of the standard we adopt. [S1]

Use MCP for discovery/data/draft preparation and a separately authorized execution interface. Define this from the target architecture, not a migration plan for the demo. Before the SDK is called AP2-compatible, pin a revision, map its required proofs and roles, and test conformance; do not put unrelated permissions in a payment mandate and call that an extension.

## 4. Better modeling: connection, access, automation, and spending are separate

These can share a contract UI without becoming one undifferentiated permission.

| Concept | Example | Does not imply |
|---|---|---|
| **Identity** | This is the authenticated Vista user | Ownership of every external account with the same phone number |
| **Connection** | This user linked this particular Eitala/Google account | All accounts at that provider are connected |
| **Access grant** | Read holdings; read the selected calendar | Permission to trade or run scheduled tasks |
| **Automation rule** | Fetch a summary on a specified schedule | Permission to buy, sell, send, or share anything |
| **Action authorization** | This exact order, or a future bounded mandate | Authority beyond its account, terms, limit, and expiry |
| **Credential** | Token used by infrastructure to reach the provider | A user decision or an independent source of Vista authority |

For financial execution, the checks are cumulative: **connected account + sufficient upstream access + valid action authorization + provider/platform eligibility and limits**. Scheduled work additionally requires an automation rule. All checks are enforced outside the model.

An access token can refresh while the user is absent without enabling scheduled activity or delegated spending. Revoking access stops dependent calls; revoking automation stops scheduled work without necessarily disconnecting the account. The UI can explain these as contract terms, not expose internal OAuth vocabulary.

## 5. Consent and account UX: fewer screens, not hidden authority

### “Consent only in contracts”

**For cooperating services, yes.** Put the requested data access and any ongoing permissions in the first relevant contract, or a connection contract when no transaction is being made. Show them as distinct terms; do not add a second generic permissions dialog. The identity/authorization service issues the scoped credential from that recorded consent, and the partner accepts it under the integration agreement.

For Google or another independent provider, Vista cannot suppress a provider-required authorization page. Present one connection journey and avoid an additional Vista confirmation that merely repeats the same decision; retain a record linked to the connection contract. Account linking or KYC may still require proof—the contract is the consent surface, not a substitute for identity verification.

### “Why not grant all Google scopes at once?”

**Bundling selected features is reasonable; requesting every permission is not.** Google’s policy requires the smallest scopes needed for functionality knowingly chosen by the user. It is not just my UX preference. [S2]

Offer one “Connect Google” step with a clear package of the features Vista actually supports. If the user chooses Calendar and documents together, request their necessary scopes together where Google supports it. Do not include deleting Drive files or sending email just to avoid a hypothetical later prompt. Wider scopes also increase verification burden and the consequence of a compromised credential. Additional scopes are requested only when a genuinely new feature is selected; partial grants must work. **COMMENT**: OK. so we request all features that we have.

### “How do multiple accounts work?”

Keep one provider/app row, with an **Accounts** section:

- **Personal** — provider-confirmed account label; selected calendar; default for personal actions.
- **Work** — separate grant; separate calendar; separately disconnectable.
- **Add another account** — another provider authorization, not a copied token.

For reads, the user can select one account or explicitly combine several; results remain labeled. For writes, bind the selected account and destination to the proposed action and display them before authorization. Ask only when neither the request nor a saved default resolves the choice. Never silently fall back to another account when the intended one fails.

### Passkeys and reassigned phone numbers

Yes: let users protect the stable Vista account with a passkey or an independent second factor. **It works only if SMS-only login and SMS-only recovery cannot bypass that protection.** Factor removal and number changes need equivalent protection; loss of the phone requires a recovery method independent of the same number. A recycled number must not automatically inherit the old account, linked services, or mandates.

Optional protection does not protect users who never enabled it. For financial-account linking, I recommend requiring an independent factor or equivalent partner-backed assurance; this is a proposed launch rule, not something already approved in your comments. **COMMENT**: when we integrate with Bank Sina, we can tell user to go to a bank Sina branch and withdraw their funds.

| Browse many episodes to find something for a commute | “Find a Persian history episode under thirty minutes”; show options and open the publisher’s episode | **Podcasts: curated RSS feeds first**, Podcast Index if broader search is needed [S5] |
| Check several appointments or manually copy an appointment message | Show selected calendars together; prepare the appointment with the correct timezone/account | **Calendar: optional**, if pilot users already use it |
| Keep receipts/files across chat and folders | Save a user-selected document, extract relevant fields, and retrieve it later | **Small Vista document workspace**, not a Google Docs replacement |
| Read news and look up unfamiliar terms | Summarize sourced reports and explain terms through Wikipedia | **News/search + Wikipedia: supporting utility**; news is not a trading oracle |

For podcasts, public feed access does not automatically grant rights to transcribe or republish full episodes. Start with permitted metadata and publisher playback links. For documents, a narrow file/receipt/notebook surface is plausible; collaborative editing, sharing, version history, and full office-suite compatibility are a different product and should not become launch dependencies. Amoozim remains a partner candidate for finding and managing real learning activities—not an assumed connection between every educational app.

**Spotify needs a specific caveat.** Its current developer policy prohibits ingesting Spotify Content into an AI model, not just training on it. Its normal development mode is limited to five authorized users and requires the app owner to have Premium; wider access needs approval. Its “Building with AI” guide concerns coding integrations, not blanket permission to send catalog/listening data into a model. Keep the requested Spotify experience on the candidate list, but validate a permitted design and access route before promising AI recommendations over Spotify data. [S4]

**Do not add all of these at launch.** Finance is the proposed acquisition/retention story; media and scheduling are selected conveniences, not evidence of financial network density. Pick complements from the first cohort’s current habits. IMDb, Spotify, and podcasts can remain planned integrations without delaying the first working financial loop.
